Haitian Rappers in Toronto and Across Canada: Young Hadene and the New Generation

Published October 7, 2026 · Updated October 22, 2026 · Toronto · By Young Hadene Team

Most searches for Haitian rappers in Toronto turn up a short list. There's a good reason for that. The story of Haitian Canadian hip-hop runs mostly through Montreal, and the numbers explain why. This guide begins with that data, moves through Montreal, Ottawa and Toronto, and ends with Young Hadene, who is part of the Toronto side of the story.

A note on who's writing: this site is run by Young Hadene's team. Every fact below about other artists links to a public source. Where something about Young Hadene can only come from him, we've marked it.

Start with the census

According to Statistics Canada, 178,995 people in Canada reported Haitian origins in the 2021 Census, and almost four in five of them lived in Montréal. Ontario was home to 17,930. Source: StatCan, July 2025

In the Montréal metro area, Haitian is the seventh most commonly reported ethnic or cultural origin, at 142,535 people or 3.4% of the population, and the median age of that group is 32.0. Source: StatCan Focus on Geography, Montréal CMA

The migration behind those figures has a history. Wikipedia's Haitian Canadians article dates Haitian immigration to Quebec from 1963. It notes that Haiti was the second-largest source of immigrants to Quebec in 1973 and that, as of 2016, more than 86% of Haitian Canadians lived in Quebec.

Toronto's Caribbean population as a whole is much larger than Montréal's: 314,795 people of Caribbean origin in the Toronto metro area, compared with 200,120 in Montréal, according to the same StatCan piece. But a large Caribbean population isn't the same thing as a large Haitian one, as the Ontario figure above shows.

Two languages, two influences

Wikipedia's overview of Canadian hip-hop sums the split up in one line. English Canadian hip-hop "tends to be influenced by Jamaican, Trinidadian and Bahamian styles," while francophone hip-hop from Quebec "is commonly influenced by French Antillean, Dominican or Haitian music."

Language is a big part of that. Haitian Creole (kreyòl ayisyen) is a French-based creole spoken by more than 13 million people. Its grammar draws on West African languages, and it isn't mutually intelligible with standard French. Wikipedia names Canada, "particularly Quebec," among the places it's spoken, and lists Montreal among the larger Creole-speaking communities outside Haiti.

Rap in Creole has its own history. According to Wikipedia's article on Haitian hip-hop, rap kreyòl began in Haiti in the early 1980s. Master Dji's 1982 track "Vakans" is described as one of the first rap songs in Creole.

Montreal: where the history is deepest

Muzion. Muzion formed in 1996 and was made up of rappers of Haitian origin from the Villeray–Saint-Michel–Parc-Extension borough: Dramatik, Imposs and his sister J-Kyll. The group won the Félix Award for Hip Hop Album of the Year at the ADISQ gala twice, the first time in 2000 for their debut, Mentalité Moune Morne. They worked with Wyclef Jean on his French-language song "24 Heures à vivre". In February 2026, Canada Post featured Muzion on a stamp in its Black History Month collection.

Imposs. After Muzion, Imposs (Stanley Rimsky Salgado) launched a solo career with Mon Poing D'Vue. The album was nominated for Album of the Year at the 2008 ADISQ Awards. Wikipedia says he is well known for coining "Real City" as a name for Montreal.

Yvon Krevé. Yvon Krevé is a Quebec rapper of Haitian origin. His 2000 debut, L'accent grave, topped Quebec sales charts and won best francophone album at the Canadian Music Awards. In 2009, La Presse named it one of five albums that had defined Quebec rap.

Roi Heenok. Roi Heenok (Henoc Beauséjour, born 1973) is a Montreal rapper, producer and entrepreneur of Haitian descent.

The same diaspora has also produced artists outside rap. Wikipedia's List of Haitian Canadians includes the electronic musician and producer Kaytranada.

Ottawa: an English-language example

DL Incognito (Oliver Nestor) is a rapper and producer from Ottawa, of Haitian and French-Canadian descent. Wikipedia says he was nominated for a Juno for Best Rap Recording in 2007 and in 2009, and he ran an independent label, Nine Planets Hip Hop, with his brother. His career shows that Haitian Canadian rap was never limited to French.

Toronto: a smaller Haitian community

Ontario's Haitian population, all 17,930 of it, is roughly an eighth the size of Montréal's, and the Toronto list reflects that. One well-documented name is Smiley. According to his Wikipedia page, Smiley (Alexander Eli Morand, born 1997 in Montreal) is a Toronto-based rapper of Haitian and Mexican origin, details sourced there to Complex. He signed with OVO Sound in 2021. That July, his single "Over the Top" featuring Drake reached No. 13 on the Canadian Hot 100 and No. 57 on the Billboard Hot 100.

Toronto belongs to the English-language side of the split described above, the side Wikipedia links to Jamaican, Trinidadian and Bahamian styles. So Haitian artists here are working in a different scene from the French-language one that produced Muzion. For the background, read the Toronto rap scene and what Toronto drill music is.

Where Young Hadene fits

His official bio describes him as a drill and dark trap artist based in Toronto. The bio says he was born in Haiti and spent part of his childhood in Florida.

Here's what the public record shows:

  • Releases. Apple Music lists Sacrifices & Success (nine tracks, January 2025) and They Took Me Down (11 tracks, October 2, 2026), plus the earlier nine-track Different Breed (2024). Most of his catalogue there carries a ℗ str8hitsrecords credit.
  • Video. His YouTube channel (@Young_Hadene) uploads drill and trap videos in English. One example is "4 Minutes of Hell", a no-hook freestyle filmed outdoors.
  • Language. We haven't checked whether any of his songs use Haitian Creole, so we're not saying they do.

To hear him, start with "Broken". For his own account of the Haiti-to-Toronto story, read Young Hadene's Haitian–Toronto story.

Why this matters for listeners

If you want to explore Haitian Canadian rap, the data points you to Montreal first. Muzion's catalogue and the Quebec artists above make a strong starting point in French. In English, try DL Incognito out of Ottawa. In Toronto, the Haitian community is smaller, which makes the artists who are here easier to miss.
