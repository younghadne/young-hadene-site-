In the Cut by Young Hadene: A Timestamped Breakdown

Published October 22, 2026 · Music · By Young Hadene Team

"Low key, moving silent." That's how the YouTube description sums up "In the Cut," which Young Hadene posted on May 20, 2026, alongside several other videos. It goes on to say that his Toronto drill "stays underground." The video itself is mostly greenery. It was shot in summer on dirt trails and by a large stretch of open water, with the camera usually in his own hand.

Below is a timestamped table first. After it come notes on what the structure shows. We haven't printed any lyrics. There's no official lyric sheet, and YouTube's auto-captions are too unreliable to quote. We only used the captions to see when vocals are present and when a block of words repeats.

Notes on the structure

The hook is easy to find

Wikipedia's article on the hook in music quotes a definition of it as the part of a song that "keeps recurring." We checked the auto-captions for any run of six words that appears more than once. In "In the Cut", one block of words turns up around 0:18–0:29, again around 1:18–1:29, and a third time around 2:25–2:34. Those three windows line up with the three stretches where the bass pulls back. The song is therefore built in a familiar way: hook, verse, hook, verse, hook, outro.

That makes a useful comparison with "4 Minutes of Hell", uploaded the same day. Its own description says it has "no hook". The same six-word check found no repeated block in that song, only one short phrase repeated within a few seconds. Here it finds a clear one. Automatic captions mishear words, so this is a strong indication, not proof.

The bass is doing the arranging

You can follow the song's sections just by listening for the low end. In the verses the sub-bass carries roughly a quarter of the measured energy. In the hook sections it drops close to zero. That's an arrangement choice: the hooks open up and the verses press harder. A beat-tracking script estimated the tempo at roughly 117–123 BPM. Automated tempo detection often gets drill wrong by halving or doubling the real figure, so treat that number as approximate.

The title fits the footage

The upload's own description, "low key, moving silent", is the only explanation of the title we have. The footage matches it: overgrown trails, thick tree cover and a shoreline with nobody else in frame. We're reading this from the description and the footage, not from the lyrics. We haven't verified what the phrase means in the song itself.

A one-person camera

Many shots are framed at arm's length, with the lens tilting and swinging as he walks. That suggests he filmed much of it himself, or a single operator stayed very close. Wardrobe doesn't change. He wears the same striped maroon-and-navy rugby-style shirt and a crossbody bag throughout. A can of drink keeps appearing in his hand.

Where it was filmed

The water in the opening and around 0:36 and 2:24 is large enough that the far shore isn't visible. It's tempting to assume it's Lake Ontario, but nothing in the upload says so, and we don't want to guess.

What we're deliberately leaving out

  • Producer credit. The upload doesn't name a producer, and we won't add one until Young Hadene confirms it.
  • Lyrics and themes. Like a lot of drill, which Wikipedia describes as often confrontational in its lyrics, the song's vocabulary is rough. We don't reproduce or paraphrase it here.
  • Release history. We didn't find "In the Cut" in Apple Music's public catalogue search, so we can't say whether it has a streaming release or when it was recorded.
  • Video crew. The upload doesn't credit a director or editor, so we haven't named one.

Watching tips

If you only have a minute, jump to 0:30. That's where the bass comes in and the first verse starts. For the contrast between sections, play 1:10–1:40: the end of verse 1, the second hook with the bass pulled out, and the bass coming back at about 1:36.
