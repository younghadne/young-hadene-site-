We Asked Toronto for Their Realest Hip Hop Hot Takes

October 7, 2026 · By Young Hadene

Ask ten people in Toronto who runs hip hop right now and you will get eleven answers. That is the 6ix. Everybody has a take, everybody is sure theirs is the realest, and the debates never actually end — they just move from the barbershop to the timeline to the studio.

So we leaned into it. Conversations across Scarborough, Rexdale, downtown, and the east end, plus late-night studio talk, surfaced the loudest debates in Toronto hip hop right now. Here are the realest hot takes, and where Young Hadene lands on each one.

Hot take 1: Toronto sounds the same now.

The argument goes that melodic drill and moody 808s took over so completely that half the city raps like the same playlist. There is some truth in it — the dark trap wave Young Hadene rides is everywhere. But the counter is stronger: the sound got big because it works, and the artists who last are the ones bending it. Young Hadene's position is simple. The Haitian-Toronto fusion — kompa rhythm memory, Creole cadence, drill drums — is proof the template still has rooms nobody has walked into yet. Same tools, different blood.

Hot take 2: Drake both built and boxed in the city.

Nobody serious denies what Drake did for Toronto. Global doors opened because of him. The hot take is the second half: that his shadow made the industry treat every Toronto rapper as either the next Drake or nothing. Ask the underground and you will hear it constantly — Jazz Cartier, Sean Leon, 88GLAM, and the whole 2005 to 2018 lineage had to fight for oxygen in that shadow. Young Hadene's read is respectful but blunt. Take the doors, skip the comparisons. The 6ix underground does not need permission to be its own thing.

Hot take 3: Lyrical miracle rap is dead in the 6ix.

The drill era rewards vibe, cadence, and presence over bar-for-bar gymnastics, and plenty of heads say the pen game suffered for it. The pushback: the pen did not die, it changed shape. Space-driven delivery, structured cadence shifts, hooks that carry weight — that is writing too, just built for 808s instead of boom bap. Listen to how Young Hadene stacks a verse and the debate answers itself.

Hot take 4: The underground matters more than the industry.

This one barely qualifies as hot anymore. Scarborough basement sessions, Rexdale cyphers, Danforth stages — the city's real A&R has always been the streets and the timeline, not label offices. The tracks defining Toronto in 2026 broke on YouTube and Spotify playlists before any executive heard them. On this take, there is no debate left. The underground is the industry now.

Hot take 5: Toronto drill is a Caribbean story now.

Dancehall, reggae, afrobeats, and Haitian traditions are not influences on the edges of the sound anymore — they are the engine. Young Hadene sits right at the center of this one. Haitian-born, Toronto-raised, making dark trap that carries both worlds. If the next era of the 6ix has a face, it looks like this fusion.

The verdict? Toronto hip hop is healthiest when it argues with itself. Every one of these takes has truth in it, and every one of them falls apart the moment a great song drops. So keep debating — and keep score by the music.
