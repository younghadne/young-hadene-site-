Toronto Rappers to Watch in 2026

Every year there's a new list of Toronto rappers to watch, and most of them recycle the same names or rank artists with no explanation. This guide takes a different approach. It covers the established names that shaped the city, how to spot rising and underground Toronto rappers yourself, where to look across the GTA, including Brampton and the wider Canadian scene, and one independent artist we can speak to directly.

The foundation

You can't talk about Toronto rap without the artists who made the world pay attention. Drake is the obvious one: he turned Toronto, "the 6ix," into a global reference point. The Weeknd and PartyNextDoor helped define the moody, atmospheric side of the city's sound. Earlier generations of Toronto and Canadian hip-hop laid the groundwork long before streaming.

On the drill side, artists from Toronto neighbourhoods built the local scene in the 2010s and gave it its own identity, separate from UK and Chicago drill. For that history, read Toronto Drill Music: The Complete Guide.

Beyond Drake, Canada has many artists worth knowing, from Toronto and from other cities. If you've only heard the biggest name, there's a lot more to find.

Why "who's the best Toronto rapper right now" has no single answer

"Best" depends on what you value: lyrics, hooks, consistency, live shows, influence, or numbers. A drill fan and an R&B-rap fan will give different answers, and both can be right. A better question is: who's making music that's distinct, consistent and honest? That's what this guide focuses on.

How to spot rising Toronto rappers

You don't need to wait for a list. Look for these signs:

A recognizable voice. Could you pick them out of a playlist without seeing the name? That matters more than anything.

Consistency. Do they release regularly? Artists who build steadily tend to last longer than those with one viral moment.

A world around the music. Strong visuals, a clear look and cover art that fits the sound.

Real engagement. Comments from people who actually listen, not just numbers.

Growth. Is each release better than the last?

Where to find underground and new Toronto rap artists

  • YouTube freestyle and performance series. Many artists get their first real audience this way.
  • SoundCloud and Audiomack. Smaller artists often post here first.
  • Independent playlist curators. Look for playlists focused on Toronto, Canadian drill or the GTA underground that are updated regularly.
  • Local shows and open mics. Openers on small bills are where you hear people early. See Toronto Hip-Hop Shows, Venues & Open Mics.
  • Credits. Look up who produced a track you like and who else they work with.

For more on keeping up week to week, read Latest Toronto Drill Songs & Releases.

Beyond downtown: Brampton, Mississauga and the GTA

The Toronto scene doesn't stop at the city limits. Brampton, Mississauga and the rest of the GTA have produced well-known artists and have active scenes of their own. Brampton in particular has a large South Asian and Caribbean community, and both show up in the music coming out of it. When you're digging for new artists, include the suburbs. A lot of the "Toronto sound" is made outside Toronto proper.

Canada more broadly

Emerging Canadian hip-hop isn't only a Toronto story. Montreal has a strong French-language and bilingual scene. Vancouver, Ottawa, Winnipeg and other cities have their own artists and styles. If you're interested in how they compare, read Toronto Rap Scene 2026: Sound, Culture, History.

An independent artist to know: Young Hadene

Underrated isn't a genre

A lot of lists call artists "underrated" because they don't have big numbers yet. That's fine, but numbers aren't the point. The artists worth watching are the ones whose music holds up when you listen closely. Give a newer artist's full project a real listen before deciding.

How to support artists you discover

  • Follow them on streaming platforms. Follows affect what algorithms recommend.
  • Add songs to your own playlists.
  • Share tracks with friends directly.
  • Go to their shows.
  • Buy merch if they have it.

For independent artists, these small actions make a real difference.

FAQ

Who are the biggest rappers from Toronto? Drake is the most famous. The city has also produced many other major artists across rap and R&B, including The Weeknd and PartyNextDoor.

How do I find new Toronto rappers? YouTube freestyle series, SoundCloud, Audiomack, independent playlists and local shows.

Is Brampton part of the Toronto rap scene? Yes. Brampton and the wider GTA are a big part of it.
