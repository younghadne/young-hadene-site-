Rise Again and From the Bottom: Young Hadene's March 16 Uploads

Published October 22, 2026 · Music · By Young Hadene Team

On March 16, 2026, two new videos went up on Young Hadene's YouTube channel within the same day: "Rise Again" and "From the Bottom". That shared upload date is the only connection between them that we can document. Neither upload calls them a set, and we don't either. Each one is covered below on its own terms, followed by a short list of facts that can be checked side by side.

As with our other song pages, we don't quote lyrics. There's no official lyric sheet, and YouTube's auto-captions aren't accurate enough to quote. We used the captions only to see when a voice comes in and whether a block of words repeats.

Song A: "Rise Again"

Listing: "Young Hadene – Rise Again (Motivational Hip Hop)". The upload runs 3:07 and YouTube files it under the Entertainment category. It has no description text.

What's on screen

The first seven seconds or so show the Str8 Hits Records logo card: chrome lettering, a silhouetted figure and a CD graphic. After a beat of black at about 0:08, the rest of the video is a single still illustration. A man in a black puffer jacket and a baseball cap climbs a golden staircase into bright, glowing clouds. "YOUNG HADENE" runs across the top and "RISE AGAIN" across the bottom. The video cycles between three crops of that artwork: from behind, a wider view of the stairs, and a close side profile. That rotation continues to the end, so it's best described as a visualizer, not a filmed music video.

We don't know how the artwork was made, and we won't guess.

How the sound is built

We scanned the audio in three-second windows, measuring overall level and how much energy sits in the low bass (below about 120 Hz).

  • 0:00–0:11: A short, bass-heavy sound plays under the logo card.
  • About 0:12–0:29: A quiet opening, up to roughly 15–20 dB below the main body. The captions pick up a voice briefly at about 0:14, then mostly music until about 0:30.
  • About 0:30–2:53: The main body, with vocals and a full beat throughout. The level stays fairly even, with two noticeable dips at about 1:30 and 2:33.
  • About 2:54–3:07: A gradual fade to the end.

The auto-captions register roughly 325 words of voice. Our check for any run of six words appearing twice found nothing. That suggests "Rise Again" doesn't rely on a repeated chorus, or that any repeat is short or sung in a way the captions don't catch. A beat-tracking script estimated the tempo at about 92 BPM, much slower than Young Hadene's May 2026 drill uploads. Automated tempo readings can be off by half or double, though.

Song B: "From the Bottom"

Listing: "Young Hadene – From The Bottom (Inspirational Trap)". The upload runs 1:53, in YouTube's Music category.

What's on screen

It opens on the same Str8 Hits Records logo card, then a short black frame at about 0:08. From about 0:12 there's a different still illustration. A figure seen from behind, in a brown puffer jacket, wears a heavy chain whose lettering reads "YOUNG HADENE". He looks down a narrow, wet alley lit by neon signs on one side and a bright orange sunset glow at the far end. "FROM THE BOTTOM" is printed across the top. Again, the video cycles between three crops of the image: a medium shot, a wider shot and a tighter shot of the shoulders and chain.

How the sound is built

  • 0:00–0:11: The logo card, with the same bass-heavy opening sound as "Rise Again". The bass readings for these first seconds are almost identical in the two uploads, which suggests a shared label intro.
  • About 0:12: There's no quiet build-up. The beat comes in at full weight straight away, and the voice starts about a second later.
  • About 0:13–1:32: The main body. A run of words heard at about 0:13–0:20 comes back at about 1:21–1:28. That's what a hook looks like in caption data: it opens the song and returns near the end. Short dips at about 0:36 and 1:30 suggest the beat drops out for a moment between sections.
  • About 1:33–1:53: The bass disappears and the track fades over its last twenty seconds.

The captions register roughly 200 words of voice. A beat-tracking script estimated the tempo at about 144 BPM. As with "Rise Again", that's an approximation.

On the "trap" in the title: Wikipedia describes trap as a hip-hop subgenre from the Southern United States, built on synthesized drums, complex hi-hat patterns and bass drums tuned to ring out as bass, a sound that goes back to the Roland TR-808. The steady low end we measured from 0:12 fits that label, but the label itself comes from the upload title, not from us.

The table shows that these are two quite different records. One is long and slow, with a patient build-up and no obvious chorus. The other is short and fast, opening on its hook and running at about 2 dB louder overall. Apart from the date, what they share is packaging: the same label intro and the same still-artwork visualizer format.

What we couldn't verify

  • Streaming releases. Neither title turned up in our search of Apple Music's public catalogue.
  • Credits. Neither upload names a producer, engineer or artwork designer.
  • Why the same day. Neither listing says why they went up together, and we haven't assumed a reason.
