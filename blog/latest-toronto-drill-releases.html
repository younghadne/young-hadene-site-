Latest Toronto Drill Songs & Releases 2026

New Toronto drill songs come out every week, and most of them never reach a big editorial playlist. If you want to hear what's actually happening in the city, you need a few reliable habits rather than one list that's out of date by next Friday. This guide covers how to find new songs and releases, how to build your own playlist, what to look for in singles, albums, freestyles and videos, and one recent independent album worth starting with.

Why a static "best of" list doesn't work

Toronto's scene moves fast. A single can blow up from a freestyle clip, and an album can drop with almost no warning. Most "top songs this week" articles are either recycled or built from the same handful of names. Rather than give you a list that ages badly, here's how to keep up yourself.

Where new Toronto drill drops first

Release day (Friday). Most music goes up on streaming services on Fridays. Check the new-release sections of Spotify and Apple Music, and the "Release Radar" style playlists that update based on who you follow. Following artists directly is the single best thing you can do: it trains the algorithm toward the scene.

YouTube. In drill, YouTube is often more important than audio streaming. Music videos, visualizers and freestyle series are where a lot of songs first get heard. Subscribe to artists and to the channels that run freestyle and performance series.

SoundCloud and Audiomack. Smaller and newer artists often post here first, sometimes before a song is officially distributed. If you want to hear things early, these platforms are worth digging through.

Social clips. Short vertical clips on TikTok, Instagram Reels and YouTube Shorts often decide which songs catch on. A 15-second snippet can create demand for a full release.

How to build your own Toronto drill playlist

  1. Start with a seed. Pick a few songs from the city you already like.
  2. Use "radio" or "song radio." On most platforms you can start a station from a track. It'll pull similar songs, including smaller artists.
  3. Check who's on the credits. Look up the producer of a beat you like and see who else they work with.
  4. Search playlists, not just songs. Search "Toronto drill," "Canadian drill" and "6ix drill" and follow the playlists that update regularly. Check the "last updated" date and skip dead ones.
  5. Prune it. Every few weeks, drop what you skip. A playlist you actually listen to beats a long one you don't.

Singles

Singles are where most drill lives. Artists drop often to stay in front of listeners, so the strongest way to judge a new single is simple: do you want to play it again? Beyond that, listen for:

  • A hook or a line that sticks after one listen
  • A beat that doesn't sound like a default type beat
  • A voice and cadence you could pick out of a playlist

Albums and projects

Full projects are rarer in drill than in other rap, which makes them more meaningful. An album shows whether an artist can hold attention for more than three minutes: sequencing, range, and whether there's a story across the tracks.

One recent example from the independent side is They Took Me Down by Young Hadene, an 11-track album released October 2, 2026. Young Hadene has Haitian roots, spent part of his childhood in Florida and is based in Toronto, and the album mixes drill rhythm with dark trap atmosphere and melodic hooks. It was covered by Exposed Vocals. If you want more after that, his singles "No Sleep," "6ix Side," "Pressure" and "After Dark," plus the album Sacrifices & Success, are all on the music page. To see where the names land, read the Toronto drill artists ranking.

Freestyles

Freestyle series on YouTube are one of the ways Toronto artists get noticed. A good freestyle shows an artist's pen and delivery without the help of a big hook or a polished mix. When a freestyle takes off, it often turns into a full single later. If you see a clip circulating, check whether a studio version exists.

Music videos

Visuals matter more in drill than in almost any other genre. A strong video gives a song a face and a setting, and a lot of fans discover songs through the video first. You don't need a big budget to make an impact. Some of the most-watched drill videos are simple: the artist, their people, and their neighbourhood. When a video goes viral, the audio streams usually follow.

How to tell if a release is worth your time

  • Replay value. Did you go back to it without thinking?
  • Identity. Could you tell who it is without seeing the name?
  • Writing. Is there a real line in there, or just filler?
  • Production. Does the beat do something specific, or could any rapper be on it?

For more on what makes the writing work, see How to Write Drill Lyrics & Hooks. For the production side, read How to Make Toronto Drill Beats.

Upcoming albums

Release dates in rap change all the time, and many drill projects are announced only days before they come out. The most reliable way to know what's coming is to follow artists directly on streaming platforms and social media, and to turn on notifications for the ones you care about. Pre-save links, when an artist posts one, also add the album to your library automatically on release day.

FAQ

When do new drill songs from the city come out? Most releases go up on Fridays, but YouTube videos and SoundCloud uploads can drop any day.

What's the best Toronto drill playlist? The best one is the one you build and keep updated yourself. Start from songs you like, use song radio, and prune it regularly.

Where can I hear Young Hadene's newest album? They Took Me Down is linked from the music page, on Spotify, Apple Music, YouTube and SoundCloud.
