Saukrates' "Fades Away (2026 Remix)": A Toronto Record Built Across Generations

This Friday, Toronto hip-hop has a record that reaches back as much as it moves forward. Saukrates, the Toronto rapper, producer and songwriter, has released "Fades Away (2026 Remix)" featuring Drake, the late King Reign and The BLK LT$. According to That Eric Alper's report from October 22, the remix arrives on October 23, 2026 through SRG-ILS, with production by Walter Milsapp III and Xman.

This post covers what the release is, who is on it, and why a remix like this is worth paying attention to if you care about how Toronto's sound gets passed from one generation of artists to the next. We're writing as fans and as an independent artist's team. We have no connection to anyone on this record, and nothing here should be read as one.

The report describes the track as an update of one of Saukrates' most meaningful collaborations, and as a recording that brings several generations of Toronto talent together. Saukrates summed it up in four words: "This is Toronto personified." He dedicated the remix to King Reign, whom he called a longtime friend and collaborator.

Remembering King Reign

King Reign, born Kunle Thomas, has passed away. The report describes him as an important figure in Canadian hip-hop, respected for his lyricism and his distinct approach, and it says his presence on the remix gives it a weight that goes beyond revisiting a familiar song.

We won't add anything to that. The respectful thing is to let the music and the people who knew him speak. If you're hearing his voice for the first time through this release, the best tribute is to go and find the rest of his work.

Why a cross-generation remix matters

Most remixes are about adding a new name to a song that's already working. This one is built differently. The report places Saukrates as a founding member of The Circle, the Toronto collective it associates with Kardinal Offishall, Choclair and Jully Black, at a time when Canadian rappers faced real barriers to mainstream recognition. Drake, on the other hand, belongs to the era when Toronto became part of the international conversation about hip-hop. Putting those chapters on one track is a statement about continuity.

That matters because scenes don't appear out of nowhere. The report makes the point that artists in Saukrates' early circle shared ideas, studio time and audiences, and that this groundwork helped build a scene that later reached listeners around the world. Whether or not you grew up on those records, the city's current sound sits on top of that work.

A few things stand out to us about how this release frames that history:

  • It honours someone who isn't here. A dedication to King Reign turns the remix into a tribute as well as a release.
  • It credits the builders, not only the stars. The report spends as much time on The Circle and on Saukrates' production and collaboration history as it does on the headline feature.
  • It treats Toronto as a collective. Saukrates' own framing, that the city finds strength in numbers, is about sharing rather than competing.

Saukrates' range, briefly

If you only know Saukrates by name, the report fills in a career that stretches well past rap verses. It describes him as a classically trained violinist who performed with the Scarborough Schools Symphony Orchestra, the lead voice of the funk band Big Black Lincoln, and a voiceover artist whose work with the Toronto Blue Jays began in 2007. It lists collaborations with Nas, Method Man, Redman and Nelly Furtado, and says his honours include JUNO Awards, MuchMusic Video Awards and an Emmy nomination.

The report also lists what he has coming. He's preparing a collection of classic recordings, a documentary is in development, he leads the Saukrates Youth Electric Symphony Orchestra (SYESO) with 60 student musicians, and he's working with Choclair on a new group called AK416, with new music expected in 2027.

We're listing these because they're useful context, not to make any claim about the remix's reception. We don't have streaming numbers or chart data for it, and we won't guess.

What independent Toronto artists can take from it

Young Hadene is a Toronto-based drill and dark trap artist who releases his own music. We're not on this record and we don't speak for anyone who is. But as an independent team, we look at a release like this and take a few practical lessons from it.

Know whose work you're building on. Drill and dark trap in Toronto didn't start from zero. Earlier generations argued for Canadian rap to be taken seriously in the first place. Knowing that history makes you a better listener, and a better artist.

Collaboration is how scenes grow. The report's picture of The Circle is people sharing studios, ideas and crowds. For an independent artist today, the version of that is working with local producers, videographers and other artists rather than trying to do everything alone.

A catalogue keeps working. A remix decades after a collaboration shows that good records don't expire. For an artist with a growing catalogue, older tracks are worth revisiting, re-presenting and keeping available.

Pay respect plainly. The King Reign dedication is simple and direct. When you honour someone in your music or your posts, you don't need to dress it up.
