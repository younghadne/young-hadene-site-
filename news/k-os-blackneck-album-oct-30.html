k-os Releases BLackneck, His First Full Album in Over a Decade

Updated Oct 30, 2026: BLackneck is out now. We added the release details, the tracklist and the credits confirmed by Apple Music and Billboard Canada, and a note on what the album says about Toronto rap. First published Oct 23, 2026.

k-os has a new full album out. BLackneck arrived on Friday, October 30, 2026, and it's listed on Apple Music with seven songs. Billboard Canada's exclusive interview from October 21 gave the background: it's his first full album in more than a decade, it's out on Spiritual Tech Records via The Orchard, and Drake is credited as executive producer and paid for the project.

This post covers what's confirmed about the release, how it was made, and why it's worth a listen if you follow Canadian hip-hop. It's news only. Young Hadene has no connection to k-os, Drake or anyone involved in this album, and nothing here should be read as one.

Credits we can confirm

We're only listing credits that a source states outright:

  • Executive producer: Drake. Billboard Canada is clear that his role was funding the record and encouraging k-os to make the album only he could make. He wasn't producing the beats himself.
  • Producer on "i would never.": Bob Rock, known for his work with Metallica, Bon Jovi and Aerosmith, according to Billboard Canada.
  • Project management: Matte Babel, the former MuchMusic VJ who is now chief brand officer at DreamCrew, worked on the project alongside k-os.
  • Label: Spiritual Tech Records, per the ℗ line on Apple Music.

We haven't seen a full personnel list for each track yet, so we're not guessing who played or produced what beyond this.

How Drake fits in

Billboard Canada says Drake grew up watching k-os on MuchMusic in the 2000s. The two worked together on the songs "Faith" parts one and two in the early 2010s and reconnected at a club around 2021 or 2022, after which Drake asked k-os to send him music. Asked to define the relationship, or whether he's part of OVO, k-os told Billboard Canada it's hard to put into words, then summed it up: "We inspire each other."

What a real recording budget made possible

k-os was previously signed to Universal Music Canada and to the indie label Dine Alone, according to the report, and he explains that labels rarely spend at pre-streaming levels anymore, especially on string sections and big studios. With Drake covering the costs, Billboard Canada reports that k-os was able to:

  • hire orchestras and choirs and build symphonic parts into the production, which he points to on "Still Number One";
  • bring in Bob Rock to produce the rock song "i would never.";
  • blend country and hip-hop on "OK Corral", which the report describes as a Wild West-inspired song with slide guitar over a hip-hop beat and a barn dance in the outro.

He told Billboard Canada the album pulls together the strongest parts of everything he's made, going back to the late '90s and early 2000s, when he signed his first deal and released most of his albums. The report lists OutKast, Shania Twain, Our Lady Peace and Radiohead as music he loved in that era.

The title and the setting

Billboard Canada explains that BLackneck plays on "Redneck," and that the title comes out of where the album was made: in B.C. and on his parents' farm property in Whitby, Ontario. The report also notes that k-os chose to stay in Canada throughout his career, and that being a musician here shaped this record.

Why a genre-blurring veteran matters: Toronto rap isn't one lane

k-os is one of the pioneers on our list of top Toronto rappers, and BLackneck shows why his name keeps coming up. Within seven songs you get orchestras and a choir, a rock song produced by the man behind records for Metallica and Bon Jovi, and a country and hip-hop crossover with slide guitar. Apple Music files the album under Pop, which tells you how hard it is to put in one box.

That matters for how people talk about Toronto rap. Outside the city, the conversation often shrinks the scene down to one sound. In reality, a record like this sits on the same map as the cold, 808-heavy style covered in our guide to what Toronto drill music is, and the moodier, melodic side we break down in dark trap vs drill. Those lanes sound nothing alike, and they all belong to the same city. For the longer history, our guide to the Toronto rap scene covers the earlier eras that artists like k-os helped build.

What independent artists can take from the story

Young Hadene releases music independently in Toronto, most recently the 11-track album They Took Me Down, released October 2, 2026. We're not connected to BLackneck, but as an independent team, a few points stuck with us:

Budget changes what's possible, but not the idea. Strings, choirs and a name producer cost money. The ideas behind the record, like blending country with hip-hop or naming an album after where you come from, don't. Independent artists can chase the idea at whatever scale they can afford.

A short album can still cover a lot of ground. BLackneck runs about 22 minutes. Seven focused songs is a format any independent artist can plan for.

Long relationships matter. The connection behind this album started with a fan watching TV and turned into collaborations years later. Real relationships in music build slowly.
