k-os Announces BLackneck, His First Full Album in Over a Decade, Out October 30

k-os is coming back with a full album. According to Billboard Canada's exclusive interview from October 21, BLackneck comes out next Friday, October 30, 2026, on Spiritual Tech Records via The Orchard. It's his first full album in more than a decade, and Drake is credited as executive producer and paid for the project.

This post sums up what the interview confirms about the album, how it was made, and why it's worth paying attention to if you follow Canadian hip-hop. It's news only. Young Hadene has no connection to k-os, Drake or anyone involved in this album, and nothing here suggests otherwise.

How Drake fits in

The interview is careful about Drake's role. He's credited as executive producer, but the report says he wasn't "behind the boards." His part was funding the album and encouraging k-os to make the record only he could make.

The history between them goes back a long way. Billboard Canada says Drake grew up watching k-os on MuchMusic in the 2000s, that the two collaborated on the songs "Faith" parts one and two in the early 2010s, and that they reconnected at a club around 2021 or 2022. After that, Drake asked k-os to send him music.

Matte Babel, the former MuchMusic VJ who is now chief brand officer at Drake's company DreamCrew, managed the project alongside k-os. When people ask k-os to define the relationship, or whether he's part of OVO, he tells Billboard Canada it isn't easy to put into words. He sums it up this way: "We inspire each other."

What a real recording budget made possible

The part of the interview that stood out most to us is about money and what it buys in a studio. k-os, who the report says was previously signed to Universal Music Canada and to the indie label Dine Alone, explains that labels rarely spend at the old pre-streaming level anymore, especially on string sections and big studios.

With Drake funding the album, Billboard Canada reports that k-os was able to:

  • hire orchestras and choirs and work symphonic elements into the production, which he points to on the song "Still Number 1";
  • bring in producer Bob Rock, known for his work with Metallica, Bon Jovi and Aerosmith, to produce the rock song "I Would Never";
  • blend country and hip-hop on "OK Corall", which the report describes as a Wild West-inspired song with slide guitar over a hip-hop beat and a barn dance in the outro.

He describes the album as the "best of everything" he's done, drawing on the late '90s and early 2000s, when he signed his first deal and released most of his albums. The report lists OutKast, Shania Twain, Our Lady Peace and Radiohead as music he loved in that era.

The title and the setting

Billboard Canada explains that BLackneck is a play on "Redneck," and that the title grows out of where the album was made: in B.C. and on his parents' farm property in Whitby, Ontario. The report also notes that k-os chose to stay in Canada throughout his career, and that being a musician here shaped this record.

Why it matters for Canadian hip-hop

k-os is one of the pioneers on our list of top Toronto rappers, and this album puts him back in the conversation with major support behind him. In the interview, he talks about Canadian artists as pioneers by nature, and about the responsibility older artists have to bring younger ones into the studio and teach them, even if those younger artists will one day replace them.

You don't have to agree with every claim in the interview to see why that point lands. A lot of the Toronto sound people hear today rests on artists who took risks before streaming made it easier to be heard. For more on that history, our guide to the Toronto rap scene covers the city's earlier eras.

What independent artists can take from the story

Young Hadene releases music independently in Toronto. We're not connected to this album, but as an independent team, a few points in the interview stuck with us:

Budget changes what's possible, but not the idea. Strings, choirs and a name producer cost money. The ideas behind the record, like blending country with hip-hop or naming an album after where you come from, cost nothing. Independent artists can chase the idea at whatever scale they can afford.

Long relationships matter. The connection behind this album started with a fan watching TV and turned into collaborations years later. Real relationships in music build slowly.

Mentoring goes both ways. k-os talks about older artists teaching younger ones. Here the younger artist ended up funding the older one's vision. The roles in a scene don't stay fixed.

What we don't know yet

The interview doesn't give streaming numbers, chart expectations, tour dates or a full list of collaborators beyond what's above, and we won't guess any of them. We'll update this post after October 30 if there's more confirmed information we can link.
